About PRASIT

The story behind the name

Our mission

PRASIT was founded to address a growing problem in Indian academic publishing — the proliferation of predatory conferences that collect fees without providing genuine peer review or credible indexing.

We believe BTech and MTech students who produce original, honest research deserve a platform with real reviewers, meaningful feedback, and verifiable publication records.

Peer review standards are applied consistently and are not adjusted based on submission volume.

We are India-rooted and open to international participation.

प्रसार

Prasāra — Sanskrit for the spread and dissemination of knowledge. The root from which PRASIT draws its name and its purpose.

The traditional Deepam (oil lamp) in our symbol represents the pursuit of knowledge — present at convocations and academic ceremonies across India.

Review Process

How peer review works at PRASIT

Transparency about our review process is core to our credibility

The review process
Every paper follows these exact steps
1

Desk check (1 week)

TPC Chair checks: correct format, plagiarism below 15%, within scope, double-blind anonymity maintained. Papers failing desk check are returned immediately with reasons.

2

Reviewer assignment (1 week)

TPC Chair assigns 3 reviewers per paper from the relevant track. Conflict of interest is checked — no reviewer from the same institution as any author.

3

Independent review (4 weeks)

Each reviewer independently evaluates the paper on: originality, technical quality, relevance, writing clarity, and references. Reviewers do not see each other's scores during this phase.

4

TPC discussion (1 week)

For borderline papers, TPC Chair facilitates discussion among reviewers. Final accept/reject decision is based on reviewer consensus and TPC Chair judgment.

5

Author notification (with feedback)

ALL authors — accepted and rejected — receive the full reviewer comments. Rejected authors receive constructive feedback explaining what was lacking and how to improve.

6

Camera-ready & DOI assignment

Accepted authors submit final camera-ready PDF. Each paper is assigned a permanent CrossRef DOI and published in the official PRASIT proceedings.

Our commitment: We target a 25–35% acceptance rate. This is not a quota — it is a quality standard. If submissions are of high quality, acceptance may be higher. If quality is poor, it will be lower.
Reviewer evaluation criteria
Every reviewer scores each paper against the same weighted framework
CriterionWeight
Originality25%
Technical Quality25%
Methodology20%
Results & Analysis15%
Relevance to Track10%
Presentation & Writing Clarity5%
Reviewers score each criterion independently and provide written comments. The weighted score guides the accept/reject recommendation but does not replace reviewer and TPC judgment on borderline papers.
Conflict of interest
Applied to every reviewer and TPC member on every paper

Same institution

The reviewer is currently employed at or enrolled in the same university or organization as any author.

Recent co-authorship

The reviewer has co-authored a paper with any author within the last 3 years.

Supervisor/student relationship

The reviewer has served, or currently serves, as the PhD/MTech advisor or supervisee of any author.

Personal or financial relationship

The reviewer has a close personal relationship with, or financial interest in, any author's work.

Reviewer-requested conflicts

The reviewer independently identifies and discloses any other relationship that could bias their judgment.

Committee member submissions

If a TPC or organizing committee member is an author on a submission, they take no part in that paper's review or decision at any stage.

How conflicts are handled: Before assignment, the TPC Chair cross-checks author affiliations and names against the reviewer pool. Any match on the categories above results in that reviewer being excluded from the assignment for that paper — no exceptions. Authors may also name individuals they believe should not review their paper, with a brief reason, at submission time; such requests are considered by the TPC Chair.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we get most

About PRASIT 2027
Why should I trust PRASIT?

PRASIT 2027 is the fifth edition, with four completed editions and published proceedings behind it. Beyond that track record, we've tried to make everything verifiable: our full review process is published on the Review Process page, and our policies are public and specify what is confirmed versus still in progress — for example, Scopus/Web of Science indexing, which is submitted for evaluation but not yet confirmed.

Is PRASIT 2027 indexed in Scopus or Web of Science?

Not yet confirmed, and we won't claim otherwise. Each accepted paper receives a CrossRef DOI (registration confirmed), making it citable and findable, and proceedings from prior editions are indexed in Google Scholar. On approval, the proceedings series will be submitted to the Web of Science Book Citation Index (BkCI) and to SCOPUS for evaluation and indexing — submission does not guarantee acceptance, and PRASIT will only display those logos once officially confirmed by each body. We do not claim IEEE Xplore indexing.

Is acceptance guaranteed if I pay the registration fee?

No. Every paper goes through double-blind peer review regardless of fee payment, and papers can be rejected. Registration fees cover conference operations (platform, certificates, proceedings assembly) — they are not a pay-to-publish arrangement.

Submission & presentation
Is PRASIT 2027 an online or in-person conference?

Fully online. Authors present live over video call during the 15–16 March 2027 sessions; exact platform and scheduling details will be shared with accepted authors closer to the date.

What if my paper is rejected — do I get any feedback?

Yes. All authors, accepted or rejected, receive the full reviewer comments. Rejected authors get feedback explaining what was lacking and how to improve, rather than a bare rejection notice.

Can I withdraw my paper after it's accepted?

Yes, subject to our refund schedule: full refund if withdrawn more than 30 days before the conference, 50% within 15–30 days, and no refund within 14 days of the conference. See the Policies page for full terms.

Is a fee waiver available?

Students from economically weaker backgrounds may apply for a fee waiver by writing to contact@prasit.in with their institution ID and a brief note.

PRASIT anti-predatory commitment
What separates PRASIT from a pay-to-publish operation

No pay-for-acceptance

PRASIT does not guarantee acceptance based on payment. Every paper is reviewed on merit before any registration fee is requested.

Submission separated from registration

Registration fees are only requested after a paper is accepted — never as a condition of submission or review.

No premature indexing claims

Confirmed indexing claims (Scopus, Web of Science, DBLP, etc.) are only published after official confirmation from the indexing body — never in advance. Where proceedings have been submitted for evaluation but not yet confirmed, this is disclosed as "submitted for evaluation," never presented as confirmed indexing.

No implied affiliations

PRASIT does not imply affiliation with any university, government body, or other organization unless that affiliation is real and disclosed.

Publication terms documented publicly

DOI, copyright, licensing, and open-access arrangements are published on this site — not hidden in fine print or disclosed only after payment.

Publication ethics
PRASIT follows COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics) guidelines

Authorship: All listed authors must have made a genuine intellectual contribution to the work. Honorary, gift, or ghost authorship is not permitted. The corresponding author is responsible for ensuring all co-authors have approved the final submission and agree to its publication.

Originality: Submitted work must be original and must not be under review at any other conference or journal simultaneously. Duplicate submission — submitting the same paper to two venues at the same time — will result in immediate rejection from PRASIT and notification to the other venue if identified.

Data integrity: Fabrication, falsification, or selective reporting of data is a serious breach of ethics. Authors must be able to provide underlying data upon request. PRASIT reserves the right to retract published papers if post-publication misconduct is established.

Generative AI policy
Applies to all use of generative AI tools in preparing a submission

Language assistance permitted

AI tools may assist with language editing, grammar, and clarity — this does not need special disclosure beyond acknowledging the tool was used.

AI cannot be an author

No AI tool may be listed as an author or co-author. Authorship requires accountability that a tool cannot bear.

Authors remain responsible

Authors are fully responsible for all claims, analysis, and content in the paper, regardless of what tools assisted in producing it.

Fabricated citations prohibited

AI-generated or "hallucinated" references that do not correspond to real, verifiable sources are treated as a data-integrity violation.

AI-generated media disclosed

Any AI-generated images, figures, or synthetic data used in the paper must be clearly disclosed as such.

References must be verified

Authors must personally verify that every reference in the paper exists and is accurately represented before submission.

Confidentiality preserved

Unpublished or confidential material — including other authors' unpublished work — must not be entered into AI tools that may retain or expose it.

Similarity screening
Checked at desk review before any paper enters peer review

All submissions undergo similarity screening using PRASIT's academic plagiarism detection tool. A similarity score above the permitted threshold may trigger additional examination — but the score alone does not decide the outcome. Decisions are based on the nature and source of the overlap, not on percentage alone: a paper with 16% similarity from properly cited sources and standard phrasing may pass, while a paper with 12% similarity copied verbatim from one uncited source may not.

✅

Typically proceeds

Below 15% similarity (excluding references and template boilerplate), with no single-source concentration.

⚠️

Additional examination

15–25% similarity, or any concentration in one source — reviewed by the TPC Chair before proceeding.

❌

Typically rejected

Above 25% similarity, or verbatim uncited copying regardless of overall percentage — rejected at desk check with the similarity report shared with the author.

🚫

Self-plagiarism

Submitting your own prior work without disclosure and proper citation is treated as plagiarism and results in desk rejection.

Our operational threshold is 15% as a screening trigger, not an automatic pass/fail line. Every flagged paper is examined by the TPC Chair before a decision is made.
Conflict of interest (COI) policy
Applied to all reviewers and TPC members

A conflict of interest exists when a reviewer has a professional or personal relationship with an author that could affect their objectivity. The following situations constitute a COI and require a reviewer to recuse themselves:

Same institution

The reviewer is currently employed at or enrolled in the same university or organization as any author.

Recent co-authorship

The reviewer has co-authored a paper with any author in the last 3 years.

Advisor / advisee relationship

The reviewer has served (or currently serves) as the PhD/MTech advisor or supervisee of any author.

Personal or financial conflict

The reviewer has a close personal relationship with, or financial interest in, any author's work.

Authors may declare COIs at submission time by listing names of individuals they believe should not review their paper, with a brief reason. Such requests are considered by the TPC Chair but are not guaranteed to be honored.
